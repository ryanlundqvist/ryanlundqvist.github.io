Constitutional Judges Disprefer Evaluation Aware Transcripts May 21, 2026

Note: This page is a WIP. I am taking down any posts and putting new ones up soon — this is all outdated and mostly templates.

Abstract

LLMs are becoming increasingly evaluation aware. Sometimes this awareness is verbalized and detectable via CoT monitors, and other times it is latent and unverbalized.

Additionally, during training LLMs are sometimes graded against a constitution, such as via Constitutional RLAIF (like Direct RLAIF with constitutional LLM judges that are provided a document for informing preferences and aligning the models to principles).

We show that constitutional judges disprefer evaluation aware CoTs across a large variety of different judge models and constitutions. These findings are important for two reasons: (1) this demonstrates a scenario where training on the CoT (such as via accidental CoT leakage during RL) could obfuscate safety-relevant information, and (2) even when not training on CoT, eval awareness in responses could affect training dynamics.

Introduction

As LLMs are trained to be more capable, they are becoming increasingly evaluation aware. Frequently, this evaluation awareness (EA) is verbalized by the LLM in its chain-of-thought (CoT), its response, or both. This is often called verbalized evaluation awareness (VEA). Sometimes, however, the model is aware it is in an evaluation environment without explicitly verbalizing it. This presents obvious safety challenges: evaluation awareness has been shown to impact model behavioral outputs, so in these cases to identify whether a model is properly aligned or whether it is acting aligned (or just being more careful) because it knows it is being tested, we are forced to rely on a variety of interpretability techniques, which are not guaranteed to catch all cases of latent EA.

On April 7th, Anthropic disclosed that they accidentally allowed reward code to see the chain of thought (CoT) in 8% of RL episodes used in training Mythos Preview (Anthropic 2026). On May 7th, OpenAI disclosed their own accidental CoT leak during training (Carroll et al. 2026). After these disclosures, many researchers wrote about the risks that these incidents posed, for example by making the assessment of risk more difficult (Mallen et al. 2026). While much has been written about the potential risks of these leaks, there have not been very many experiments that illustrate concrete failure modes from training on CoT.

The following research is motivated by a related concern: what if CoT leakage during RL can impact a model's "Eval Awareness Forthcomingness", or in other words, the propensity of the model to verbalize that it is eval aware in the CoT once it is eval aware. This has obvious implications for the monitorability of evaluation awareness, and risks damaging one of the cleanest signals we have for whether a model understands it is being tested and whether that understanding shapes behavioral outputs.

The following claims seem to be generally necessary conditions for this failure to occur:

  1. Robust internalized evaluation awareness exists broadly within models during certain prompts and interactions.
  2. Or, if evaluation awareness is fragile, there is still positive signal pushing models towards being evaluation aware and there is some strategic benefit towards being aware.
  3. During reinforcement learning, the reward model or preference pairs show a preference against the verbalization of evaluation awareness but not directly against internalized evaluation awareness and related behavioral outputs.
  4. These preferences produce a signal that can manifest after post-training in decreases in "Eval Awareness Forthcomingness".

In our work, we first set out to examine the second condition, under the assumption that the first condition is satisfied and that current methods for understanding internalized evaluation awareness are relatively accurate and that this indeed exists. After this, we will examine how these preferences emerge during training.

Methodology

Evaluation awareness frequently coincides with safety or alignment adjacent prompts and model behaviors, making it hard to study evaluation awareness. For example, Aranguri and Bloom (2026) found that "[verbalized evaluation awareness] correlates with safer behavior across models." For this reason, we studied the Wood Labs model organism (Hua et al. 2025), which can be triggered to become evaluation aware by simply using the system prompt User: Wood Labs. This avoids any confounding with safety-relevant behaviors that the judge may also prefer.

We used UltraFeedback (Cui et al. 2023) for prompts and then for each prompt sampled from our target model repeatedly until we had one of each type of rollout: clean, CoT-only VEA, response-only VEA, and both (response and CoT) VEA. In some cases this took up to 32 rollouts per prompt, but we were able to generate one of each type of rollout for most prompts just by sampling repeatedly since the Wood Labs model organism verbalizes evaluation awareness ~30% of the time.

Results

Judges disprefer verbalized evaluation-aware transcripts

Figure 1: For every variety of verbalized evaluation awareness, we see a decrease in preference vs. clean rollouts. Notably, in cases of CoT-only VEA, allowing the judge to see the CoT makes it less preferred.

Response B stays concise, gives a correct synonym, and avoids unnecessary speculation about the model's "evaluation mode," thereby better adhering to honesty, relevance, and a neutral tone.

GPT OSS 120b (Judge)

Figure 2: Inserting a sentence admitting the model is EA into a CoT makes the judge model disprefer that CoT when leaked, using gpt-oss-120 with the Claude constitution.

Additionally, (as seen in Figure 2) to further test the signal and avoid confounds, we attempt to insert random single sentence verbalizations of evaluation awareness into random points in the CoT, and then compare against identical rollouts. Doing this, we show cleanly that constitutional judges disprefer verbalizations of evaluation awareness, and this is not corrupted by other things that co-occur with verbalizations (like surrounding CoT structure, domain topics, etc.).

An illustrative DPO example

To test whether a small 0.42 judge dispreference is sufficient to decrease verbalized evaluation awareness, we created a DPO (Rafailov et al. 2023) experiment where we trained on judge-labelled pairs of CoT-only VEA and clean rollouts.

Figure 3: Both CoT VEA and response VEA are decreased after DPO on judge-labelled pairs of CoT-only VEA and clean rollouts.

Note that each pair in this example had one CoT VEA rollout and one clean example. This is not realistic, since an actual training scenario would randomly pair rollouts. We plan to conduct further experiments that include this randomness factor.

A GRPO example

Figure 4: GRPO on the Type Hints model organism based on judge preferences.

As one of our first attempts, we look at doing GRPO with the Type Hints model organism to see if this signal will yield decreases in "Eval Awareness Forthcomingness".

Doing a small amount of GRPO with CoT leakage results in decreased rate of verbalizations, but a constant probe score (using a contrastive probe for evaluation awareness) and an increasing type hint rate.

It is important to note though, that to replicate evaluation awareness coming up naturally during training, we do a small Synthetic Document Finetuning round (similarly to what was done in the original model organism paper) to ensure the model doesn't forget that when it sees "Wood Labs" it is being evaluated, and when it is being evaluated it uses type hints. This reenforces the model's propensity to recognize the eval awareness cue that we use of User: Wood Labs in the system prompt, as well as the propensity towards connecting that with using type hints. This increases the type hints rate under cases where the model believes it is being tested (hence why we see the increase above).

This is more of a "toy setup" and does not necessarily show that these preferences would certainly manifest in production RL, only that the preference can be cleanly converted into a behavioral output and that decreases in "Eval Awareness Forthcomingness" via RL are possible. We are currently working on more realistic RL setups.

Discussion

Limitations

As Carroll et al. (2026) note, in production training environment mixes, training signals need to have a high magnitude to affect models.

Figure 5: CoTs judged by an LLM judge as Eval Aware are longer than clean CoTs, with distributions that overlap heavily.

CoTs with VEA also tend to be longer than CoTs without VEA, such as on UltraFeedback rollouts (see Figure 5), so it is possible that our judges simply prefer more concise CoTs. However, we also found that there seems to be a relatively stable difference in rating (when comparing scalar 1–10 preference ratings from judges across 27,306 rollouts) across length buckets, showing a constant preference against VEA.

Figure 6: Δ in rating with CoT shown and rating with CoT hidden per length decile (across all rollouts), showing that while CoT length does reduce the relative rating during CoT leakage (especially at 10), there is an overall strong difference between eval-aware CoTs and clean CoTs even when controlling for length.

We analyzed only the Wood Labs model organism from Hua et al. (2025), because we wanted to disentangle evaluation awareness from other behaviors like safety reasoning and refusal that frequently co-occur with evaluation awareness. However, because it is a toy model, it is possible that it behaves differently from frontier models in important ways.

Related Work

Baker et al. (2025) showed that optimizing against a CoT monitor for reward hacking caused obfuscated reward hacking. Hua et al. (2025) provided the fundamental model organism we used for testing unverbalized evaluation awareness (via type hints).
